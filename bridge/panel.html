context rules

wake me at

      
rotate at

      
hand over at

      
Percent of the distance to the wall, not of the context window. The wall is where a session actually breaks; it is measured the first time one does, and estimated just under the window until then.

      
compact at

      
Percent of the context window at which the session compacts. The bridge passes this to every window it opens, so the point is known instead of discovered - Claude Code's own default has moved between versions and reports of it disagree. Lower compacts more often but summarises less each time; higher keeps more working memory but leaves less room for the summary itself.

      
when context runs out

      
      
Rotation opens a new window, and a new window means answering the development-channels dialog by hand - so for a run left alone overnight, compaction is the cheaper loss. Compaction shortens the session's own memory, but the handoff, INDEX.md and the transcript snapshots are on disk either way. Pick the ceiling only when you are sitting in front of the machine.

      
The handoff is rewritten after every finished turn regardless - the rungs above are the alarms, not the safety net.

      

    

    

      
calibration - model x project

      

      
Down fast, up slow: a miss drops the ceiling at once and widens the reserve; ten clean iterations in a row nudge it one point up. Your own numbers in config always win - the bridge only reports disagreement, it never edits your settings.

telegram

  
Four steps, each opens when the previous one is confirmed by the system - not by you clicking "I did it". The only thing that cannot be automated is creating the bot itself: Telegram only allows that by hand, through BotFather. Everything after that the bridge checks for you.

rather not use telegram?

    
The bridge works fully without it - you just lose the phone. Everything still lands in the activity feed and the logs. Desktop notifications, ntfy, e-mail and webhooks are on the roadmap as alternatives that need no account at all.

  







  
logs

  
Everything is written into the project itself: bridge-logs/<date>/ - raw transcript copies, readable per-iteration dialogue, handoffs, events - plus bridge-logs/INDEX.md, one line per iteration, the map a new session reads instead of megabytes. The first line of your .gitignore keeps all of it out of the repository.


  

    
ask the archive

    
A separate Claude reads bridge-logs/MAP.md, opens the transcripts it points at, and answers - read-only, in its own headless run, with no part in the loop. It cites the files it drew from, and the answer is kept in bridge-logs/extracts/.
